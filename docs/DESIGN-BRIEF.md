# Design Brief: Edwardian Botanical Game Show

**Project:** Bahá’í Game Show App  
**Repository:** https://github.com/adibbehjat/Bahai-Game-Show-App  
**Visual direction:** Early-20th-century illustrated greeting postcards, circa 1900–1920  
**Design philosophy:** Antique correspondence meets interactive game design

## 1. Creative Vision

Create a distinctive, historically inspired visual identity for an interactive Bahá’í game-show application.

The experience should resemble an early-1900s illustrated greeting postcard brought to life as a digital interface.

Draw inspiration from Edwardian stationery, botanical illustrations, lithographic printing, ornate serif typography, and the restrained elegance of antique postcards.

The aesthetic should feel:

- Historical, but not outdated in usability.
- Whimsical and inviting, but not cartoonish.
- Decorative, but never visually crowded.
- Warm, thoughtful, and dignified.
- Appropriate for participants of different ages and cultural backgrounds.

**The central design principle is that negative space is as important as the illustrations themselves.**

The application should not resemble a contemporary game show with a vintage filter. Its visual identity should originate from the design language of historical printed correspondence.

## 2. Botanical Identity

### Signature illustration: Chrysanthemums

Retain chrysanthemums as the primary botanical identity of the application.

Although chrysanthemums are associated with November, their use here is artistic rather than seasonal. They serve as a consistent, recognizable floral signature across the entire application.

Use hand-painted botanical illustrations inspired by antique chromolithographs and watercolor greeting cards.

**Botanical characteristics:**

- Fine ink outlines and softly layered pigments.
- Intricate petals with natural irregularity.
- Muted cream, champagne, ochre, rose, and terracotta tones.
- Olive and sage-green stems and foliage.
- Flower buds, individual leaves, and occasional detached petals.
- Slight imperfections associated with historical printing techniques.

Avoid photorealistic flowers, clip-art arrangements, identical repeated illustrations, or excessively polished digital gradients.

### Floral composition

Position flowers primarily along the outer boundaries of the screen.

Favor asymmetrical arrangements:

- Larger floral clusters emerging from a corner.
- Smaller branches extending from opposite edges.
- Occasional individual blossoms serving as accents.
- Substantial undecorated space around interactive content.

Flowers should frame the experience without competing with the application interface.

## 3. Historical Postcard Design Language

The application should borrow recognizable characteristics of early-20th-century postcards without literally reproducing the layout of a mailed postcard on every screen.

**Key references:**

- Edwardian illustrated greeting postcards.
- Antique botanical plates.
- Hand-tinted lithographic artwork.
- Historical correspondence stationery.
- Ornamental typography and delicate engraved flourishes.
- Lightly textured ivory paper.

Incorporate subtle features such as hairline dividers, small flourishes, delicate corner ornaments, and occasional antique-gold detailing.

Historical postcard elements such as correspondence lines, divided backs, postage frames, or embossed borders may be used sparingly when they reinforce the composition.

Avoid decorative features that imply an actual mailing address or postage requirement.

## 4. Color Palette

The default application palette should be based on warm parchment and sepia ink.

| Role | Suggested color |
|---|---|
| Main background | Parchment `#F4E9D8` |
| Light paper surface | Ivory `#FBF5EA` |
| Primary typography | Umber `#4A2E1A` |
| Secondary typography | Soft sepia `#6B4A32` |
| Antique gold | `#D9A441` |
| Olive foliage | `#566B3A` |
| Muted plum | `#6E4E6B` |
| Terracotta | `#8A4A3F` |

These colors build on the application's existing design system.

Keep saturation restrained. Paper should remain light enough to preserve readability and the feeling of open space.

Avoid heavy yellow filters, artificial staining, torn-paper effects, and excessive distressed textures.

## 5. Typography

Typography should balance historical character with modern readability.

**Display typography**

Use an expressive historical serif reminiscent of Edwardian greeting cards for the application title, game introductions, and major headings.

Decorative lettering may include elegant swashes or slightly irregular letterforms, but ornamentation should be restrained.

**Body typography**

Use a highly legible typeface for questions, explanations, buttons, instructions, and player information.

The existing Fraunces and Nunito typefaces provide a practical starting point. Consider refining the display typography without sacrificing the clarity of the interactive interface.

Ensure full support for Bahá’í transliteration and diacritics.

**Hierarchy**

- Ornate display lettering for titles.
- Classical serif for section headings.
- Clear, readable type for gameplay and controls.
- Small ornamental text only where it remains legible.

Do not use ornate calligraphy for long questions or answer choices.

## 6. Layout and Negative Space

The application should feel spacious, particularly on large displays.

Use a central content area surrounded by lightly illustrated botanical boundaries.

**Layout principles:**

- Maintain generous margins around content.
- Let the parchment background remain visible.
- Avoid unnecessary cards, nested containers, and heavy borders.
- Position botanical artwork behind or outside the primary reading areas.
- Use subtle separators instead of thick dividing lines.
- Keep the number of visible interface elements appropriate to the current task.

On welcome and transition screens, substantial negative space is desirable.

During gameplay, prioritize the size and clarity of questions, answers, and timers over decorative openness.

**The screen should never feel like every available space must be filled.**

## 7. Application Screen Designs

### Welcome screen

The welcome screen should resemble the illustrated front of a vintage greeting postcard.

Feature the application's title prominently, framed by chrysanthemums and delicate foliage.

Provide a visually quiet central area with clear actions to host or join a game.

Use minimal introductory text.

### Game lobby

Present the game lobby as an elegant piece of historical correspondence.

The room code should be visually prominent, using large, legible typography.

Place the QR code on a clean white surface, optionally framed by a delicate antique border.

Player names may appear as understated labels, resembling handwritten or printed correspondence tags.

Botanical decoration should remain secondary to joining instructions.

### Question screen

The question screen should resemble a beautifully typeset page from an antique illustrated publication.

The question occupies the primary visual area.

Answer options should be clearly separated, with restrained color accents and recognizable symbols.

Decorative flowers should stay near screen boundaries.

Maintain strong contrast and visibility when displayed on a television or projector.

### Answer reveal

When revealing an answer, use a subtle transition reminiscent of unveiling a printed illustration or turning a page.

Emphasize the correct answer through typography, borders, and clear visual indicators.

Display supporting explanations and sources in a readable, historically appropriate presentation.

Avoid excessive animation or celebratory visual effects that distract from learning.

### Scores and podium

Treat rankings as elegant printed results rather than flashy competitive graphics.

Consider typographic inspiration from antique certificates, numbered correspondence cards, and historical award programs.

Use small floral accents, thin gold rules, and restrained visual emphasis to distinguish the leading players.

The final podium may feature more elaborate botanical illustration while maintaining the application's overall dignity.

### Player mobile interface

The mobile experience should share the same design language without carrying the same amount of decoration.

Prioritize large touch targets, readable answers, and simple navigation.

Use small floral details and restrained parchment textures to maintain visual consistency.

The phone interface must remain functional on compact screens.

## 8. Occasion-Based Themes

The design must be universal and should not be tied to any particular Holy Day, season, or calendar date.

Maintain a consistent visual identity while allowing subtle thematic variations.

For example:

| Theme | Visual treatment |
|---|---|
| Default | Warm sepia and golden chrysanthemums |
| Naw-Rúz | Fresh sage and spring-green accents |
| Riḍván | Soft rose and warm botanical accents |
| Twin Holy Birthdays | Muted gold and champagne |
| Covenant-related occasions | Sage and subdued plum |
| Solemn commemorations | Restrained umber, ivory, and desaturated florals |

The design structure, typography, and botanical illustration style remain consistent.

Only supporting colors, botanical accents, and appropriate motion behaviors should change.

For solemn commemorations, use quieter imagery, reduced movement, and more restrained celebratory effects.

## 9. Motion and Interaction

Animation should feel like gentle movement within an illustrated postcard.

Suitable effects include:

- Very slow movement of botanical branches.
- Softly drifting individual petals.
- Gentle fades between screen states.
- Subtle unfolding or reveal transitions.
- A chrysanthemum-inspired countdown timer.

Avoid flashing lights, rapid movement, exaggerated bouncing, and modern arcade effects.

All decorative motion should respect reduced-motion accessibility preferences.

During reverent modes, decorative animation should become minimal or disappear.

## 10. Accessibility and Functionality

Historical styling must not compromise usability.

Preserve:

- WCAG AA text contrast at minimum.
- Large, easily activated touch targets.
- Strong typographic hierarchy.
- Clear keyboard focus indicators.
- Explicit selected, correct, and incorrect answer states.
- Information communicated through more than color alone.
- Readability at television and projector distances.
- Reduced-motion support.

The antique aesthetic should never make controls appear noninteractive.

## 11. Artwork and Asset Requirements

Prefer original hand-drawn or hand-painted illustrations, professionally digitized for web use.

Produce reusable assets rather than full-screen images with embedded text.

Required assets should include:

- Primary chrysanthemum botanical illustrations.
- Corner floral arrangements with transparent backgrounds.
- Individual petals, buds, and foliage elements.
- Subtle parchment-paper textures.
- Delicate ornamental rules and flourishes.
- Optional historical postcard-inspired decorative frames.
- Multiple color variations compatible with the application's theme system.

Assets should support responsive layouts and be reusable throughout the application.

Designers should provide editable original artwork and optimized web assets.

## 12. Integration With Existing Application

The project already contains a theme system with configurable design tokens, botanical artwork, and reusable UI components.

The redesign should **refine the existing visual system rather than replace the application's functionality**.

Relevant files include:

- `DESIGN.md` — existing design system specification.
- `public/theme/theme.css` — typography, colors, spacing, layout, and theme tokens.
- `public/theme/flower.js` — botanical artwork and animation behavior.
- `public/theme/art/` — floral illustrations, paper textures, and background artwork.
- `public/styleguide.html` — component and theme previews.

The designer should provide specifications and assets that can be incorporated into these existing structures.

The application must continue to support customizable themes, reverent modes, responsive layouts, and its existing gameplay interactions.

## 13. Final Deliverables

Provide a coherent design system that includes visual references, typography recommendations, a finalized color palette, reusable botanical illustrations, component specifications, and screen mockups.

Mockups should cover the welcome screen, lobby, projected questions, answer reveal, final podium, and mobile player interface.

Also provide guidelines for extending the design to additional Bahá’í occasions without redesigning the application.

## 14. What to Avoid

Avoid contemporary game-show aesthetics, neon colors, excessive gradients, glossy controls, cartoon flowers, generic AI-generated imagery, heavily distressed parchment, visually crowded floral borders, and elaborate ornamentation around every component.

Avoid placing historically styled decorative elements where they interfere with comprehension or gameplay.

The finished experience should not look like an old poster repurposed into a website.

It should feel like a thoughtfully designed interactive experience that naturally belongs to the visual world of early-20th-century illustrated postcards.

---

**Final creative direction**

*Imagine an Edwardian botanical postcard brought to life: delicate chrysanthemums framing warm parchment, elegant typography carrying the content, and generous negative space allowing the experience to breathe.*

*The interface should feel timeless, welcoming, and playful without losing its dignity.*

## Addendum (2026-11-07): Visual Identity, Eggshell Edition

*This supersedes the parchment and sepia direction in §4 where they conflict.*

### Visual Identity: Edwardian Botanical Postcard

Create a timeless visual identity inspired by early-20th-century illustrated greeting postcards, emphasizing eggshell-white stationery, hand-painted botanical illustrations, and elegant historical typography.

The dominant background should be a soft eggshell white, with subtle paper texture and gentle tonal variations reminiscent of high-quality antique correspondence paper.

Preserve chrysanthemums as the application's signature botanical motif, illustrated in muted champagne, golden yellow, dusty rose, and delicate sage-green foliage.

Use dark walnut-brown ink for typography, with restrained antique-gold accents for ornamental borders, fine rules, and decorative details.

The floral illustrations should emerge naturally from selected corners and edges, leaving generous negative space throughout the interface.

Avoid sepia overlays, heavily yellowed parchment, distressed paper, or excessive vintage effects. The historical character should come primarily from the illustrations, typography, paper quality, and composition rather than artificial aging.

The visual identity must remain universal across Bahá’í Holy Days and adaptable to both celebratory and solemn occasions.

**Design principle:** An elegant Edwardian postcard printed on eggshell-white stationery, with delicate botanical artwork framing a clean, functional digital experience.
