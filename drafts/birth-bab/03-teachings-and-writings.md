# Birth of the Báb: Teachings and writings (drafts)

## Categories found
- The Báb's name and station (Gate, Forerunner, Primal Point)
- Him Whom God shall make manifest
- The Bayán (Persian and Arabic), structure in unities (váḥids) and gates
- The Qayyúmu'l-Asmá' (Surih of Joseph, 111 chapters)
- The Badíʻ calendar and the number 19
- Chronology of writings (Qayyúmu'l-Asmá', Epistle of Justice, Surih of Abundance commentary, Panj Sha'n, Máh-Kú works)

## Candidates
1. easy, single: Meaning of "Báb". Answer: The Gate. Source: Wikipedia Teachings. Solid.
2. easy, single: Chief "Mother Book". Answer: The Bayán. Source: Wikipedia, Saiedi. Solid.
3. easy, truefalse: Badíʻ calendar 19 months of 19 days. True. Source: Wikipedia, Bahaipedia. Solid.
4. easy, spell: Bayán. Source: Bahaipedia, Saiedi. Solid.
5. easy, multi: True of the Báb: declared 1844, born in Shíráz, Forerunner (not Aqdas). Source: Bahaipedia. Solid.
6. easy, single: Who "Him Whom God shall make manifest" is. Answer: the Promised One, recognized as Bahá'u'lláh. Source: Wikipedia, Bahaipedia. Solid (Bahá'í view).
7. medium, single: Qayyúmu'l-Asmá' is a commentary on the Surih of Joseph. Source: Saiedi. Solid.
8. medium, truefalse: Four extra days (19x19=361, to 365). True. Source: Wikipedia, Bahaipedia. Ayyám-i-Há name is from general Bahá'í knowledge, not the sources.
9. medium, order: Declaration 1844, Persian Bayán 1847-48, execution 1850, Bahá'u'lláh's declaration 1863. Source: Bahaipedia, Wikipedia. Wikipedia says Bahá'u'lláh claimed the role in 1863; Bahá'í tradition dates it to 1863 in Baghdad (Ridván); the question says "in Iraq".
10. medium, spell: Váḥid. Source: Bahaipedia, Saiedi. Solid.
11. medium, multi: Persian Bayán: Máh-Kú, nine unities, Mother Book (not nineteen unities). Source: Saiedi, Bahaipedia. Solid. Explanation notes nineteen unities belong to the Book of Names (Saiedi).
12. hard, order: Qayyúmu'l-Asmá' 1844, Epistle of Justice Jan 1846, Surih of Abundance commentary c. May 1846, Panj Sha'n spring 1850. Source: Saiedi. Solid.
13. hard, single: Qayyúmu'l-Asmá' has 111 chapters. Source: Saiedi. Solid.
14. hard, truefalse: Last unity of Persian Bayán has ten gates. True. Source: Saiedi, Wikipedia-adjacent Bahaipedia. Solid.
15. hard, multi: Written largely in Máh-Kú: Persian Bayán, Arabic Bayán, Seven Proofs (not Qayyúmu'l-Asmá'). Source: Saiedi. Saiedi says "most" of both Bayáns; Bahaipedia dates Persian Bayán to late 1847 or early 1848.
